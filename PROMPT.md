## 🔴🔴🔴 LEAD WITH THIS — twenty service areas, and no website to rank in any of them

This is a first-website build, so there is no defect list. **The argument is opportunity, not failure** — and the opportunity is unusually concrete, because it is countable.

**Glaze lists twenty service locations across Tampa Bay.** Lutz, Largo, Tampa, Brandon, Lakeland, Bradenton, Dade City, Clearwater, Plant City, Palm Harbor, Shady Hills, Spring Hill, Zephyrhills, Land O' Lakes, Wesley Chapel, St. Petersburg, Temple Terrace, New Port Richey, Ybor City, and the Tampa–St. Petersburg metro generally.

A Google Business Profile ranks a business **where it is registered**. It does not give them a page that can rank for *"mobile detailing Wesley Chapel"*, *"ceramic coating Clearwater"* or *"paint correction St. Petersburg"* — because there is no page. For a business whose single operational advantage is that it drives to the customer, that is the whole growth lever, missing.

**So the product here is a location-page system.** Twenty real pages, one per area, each with genuinely distinct content. Not a hero, not a gallery — the location pages are the thing worth paying for, and almost no mobile detailer builds them. See §Location pages, and read the warning there: twenty real pages is a **content job the client has to resource**, and twenty find-and-replace pages are worse than none.

**Second thread — their best sentence is buried in a paragraph.** Their own GBP copy says:

> *"As a fully mobile service, we bring our expertise directly to you, equipped with **our own water and electricity**."*

Most mobile detailers need a tap and an outlet **from the customer**. That single requirement rules out apartment residents, office car parks, parking garages, condo decks, job sites and fleet yards — which is most of the people who would pay for convenience. Glaze doesn't need either, and the fact is currently a subordinate clause in a block of GBP text nobody reads to the end. **That belongs in the hero, at display size.** It is the sentence that wins apartment and commercial work, and it is free.

**Third thread — the high-ticket work is sitting in a flat list.** Ceramic coating, paint correction, PPF removal and vinyl wrap removal are multi-hundred-dollar jobs bought by a considered, researching buyer who reads before they call. On the GBP they appear in the same sentence as pet hair removal. Each needs its own page, its own explanation of what the process actually is, and its own enquiry path. (⚠️ And each carries a compliance flag — see B2, B3 and B6. Do not write a word of this section before reading the compliance block.)

**Fourth, for later in the conversation, not the opener:** their published hours read **"Open 24 hours, Monday–Saturday. Closed Sunday."** That is almost certainly a GBP setting meaning *"you can contact us any time"* — it is not a claim that they detail cars at 3am. It is fine on a GBP and it reads oddly on a website. Raise it kindly and late: on a site it becomes **booking availability**, not operating hours. See B7.

**None.** A fully mobile detailer carrying their own water and power, covering twenty areas of Tampa Bay, offering four high-ticket services and accepting four payment methods, with no website at all — that is a good business with a missing asset, which is the best kind of lead.

⚠️ **Two conditions, not walk-aways:**

### 📌 Why these apply

**Price is the first thing this buyer looks for, which is exactly why we may not guess it.** Detailing is quoted on vehicle size, paint condition, interior condition and travel, and the same "full detail" can legitimately differ threefold between a clean sedan and a pet-haired truck. A figure we invent becomes an expectation the client has to argue with at the kerb. The finished-but-empty price card is the better sales object anyway: it shows the client exactly where their number goes.

**Ceramic coating claims are the most-litigated copy in this trade.** "9H", "5 years", "permanent", "self-healing" are objective, checkable performance claims; under §5 of the FTC Act an objective claim needs substantiation **before** it runs, and the substantiation here belongs to the coating manufacturer, not to the installer. An installer who repeats a figure they cannot evidence is the one holding it. Process copy — decontamination, correction, application, cure, aftercare — converts the considered buyer better and costs nothing to defend.

**Paint correction has a physical ceiling.** Clear coat is finite; a defect deeper than the remaining clear coat cannot be polished out, and a guarantee to remove it is a promise the laws of materials will break. "Reduces the appearance of" is both honest and standard.

**Ozone is a regulatory category, not a service feature.** Ozone is a lung irritant, and the EPA has published that ozone generators sold as air cleaners are not effective at concentrations meeting public health standards. The moment copy says ozone *kills*, *sanitises* or *disinfects*, it is a pesticidal and health claim sitting under **FIFRA** and the FTC Act, and a device marketed with those claims may require EPA registration. This is the one flag in the brief where a single careless verb creates real exposure — so the rule is a banned-word list rather than a judgement call. **We are a web studio and this is not legal advice:** we state what we will not render and route the substantiation to them.

**Film removal fails on old film, not on bad technique.** Sun-baked PPF and vinyl on a Florida car, especially over a respray, can take clear coat with it. Every experienced installer knows this and assesses first; a website promising damage-free removal is writing a cheque the installer would never write in person.

**Water is two separate issues and they must not be mixed.** *"We bring our own water and electricity"* is a **convenience** fact and it is their best sentence. **Where the water goes afterwards** is a **stormwater** question under local Florida ordinances and NPDES rules, and it determines which properties will let them work. Framing containment as eco-marketing invites a Green Guides problem; framing it as commercial capability wins fleet and property-management jobs.

**"Open 24 hours" is a GBP artefact.** On a profile it signals contactability. On a website it reads as either a mistake or an invitation to call at 3am, and it undermines everything else on the page.

**No minors, and none of their photos.** Their GBP photos are customers' cars — identifiable vehicles with readable plates, shot with no release for our use — and this is a trade where a stock photo of a child in a freshly cleaned back seat is one search away and completely off-limits. The car is the hero. It always was.

## 🔴 The opportunity — the pitch list (there is no site to critique)

Eight points. The first three are the sale.

1. 🔴🔴🔴 **Twenty service areas and no page to rank in any of them.** A GBP ranks you where you are registered. It cannot rank you for *"mobile detailing Wesley Chapel"*, *"ceramic coating Clearwater"* or *"paint correction St. Petersburg"*, because those are searches that need **pages**. Twenty areas is twenty pages, and in this trade almost nobody builds them. **This is the product.**
2. 🔴🔴 **"We bring our own water and electricity" is the whole business and it's a subordinate clause.** Most mobile detailers need the customer's tap and the customer's outlet — which rules out apartments, condos, offices, garages, fleet yards and job sites. Glaze doesn't. That sentence should be the largest text on the homepage.
3. 🔴 **Four high-ticket services are in a flat list with pet hair removal.** Ceramic coating, paint correction, PPF removal and vinyl wrap removal are multi-hundred-dollar, considered purchases. The buyer reads for twenty minutes before they call. Right now there is nothing to read. Four pages, four processes explained, four enquiry paths.
4. 🔴 **There is no price anywhere, and no vehicle size tier.** Not a reason to invent one — but the absence of a sedan/SUV/truck axis means every enquiry starts with a phone call and a guess. A published tier structure filters the people who were never going to book and pre-qualifies the ones who were.
5. 🔴 **No before/after, publicly, in the one trade where before/after sells by itself.** They are doing the work. Nobody can see it. This is the cheapest asset in the business and it is unshot.
6. 🔴 **One contact channel: a phone number.** No email, no form, no text-in option stated. Every enquiry has to become a phone call at a moment that suits the customer and not the detailer — who is, by definition, usually elbow-deep in someone's door card.
7. 🔴 **A quote form could do the quoting.** Vehicle size, interior condition, service wanted, service address, access (driveway / street / garage / car park), preferred window. That is a quote they can price from their phone between jobs instead of a twelve-minute call.

## Art direction — REFLECTION. Gloss and specular light.

### The idea

A detailed car's defining quality is a **mirror finish**. And "glaze" is both a step in the detailing process and the name of a ceramic finish — the business is named after the thing we are designing.

**So the design system is gloss and specular light:**

- **Deep lacquer surfaces.** Sections read like wet paint under a single light source — dark, saturated, depth rather than flatness.
- **One hard specular highlight.** A narrow, high-contrast band of light rakes diagonally across panels, cards and the display type. **One light source, consistent angle, every time** — because a car panel reflects one sun.
- **Reflection gradients beneath elements.** Cards, the wordmark and key figures sit above a short, soft, vertically-mirrored gradient — an implied wet floor. Subtle: 12–18% opacity, 40–60px tall, fading to nothing.
- **Wet-edge section transitions.** Section boundaries are a 1px specular line with a soft bloom immediately beneath it, like the edge where water breaks on a waxed panel. ⛔ Not a glow, not a neon rule.
- **Matte → gloss as the before/after device.** The "before" state is **desaturated, flat, matte, no specular**; the "after" state is **saturated, deep, with the specular band present**. This is a real design system doing real work: the slider isn't a gimmick, it's the brand.

**Why this concept:** it is honest to the product, it is unused anywhere in the library, and **it solves the colour problem** — a gloss system needs a **dark ground and one light source**, not a loud accent. The hero of this palette is **light**, not hue, which is exactly how we avoid every blue detailing site in existence.

⛔ **Discipline, or it becomes a hologram.** **One specular band per viewport.** It appears on the display type, the hero, one card row, and the section edges — nowhere else. ⛔ No chrome-gradient fills on type. ⛔ No metallic bevels. ⛔ No lens flare, no sparkle, no star glints, no "shine sweep" animation looping forever, no carbon fibre, no water droplets as decoration, no bubbles, no suds.

### 🔴 Palette — deep lacquer green, platinum specular, one jade accent used sparingly

🔴 **DO NOT GO BLUE.** All three supplied references are blue, and blue is occupied in this folder — see §Do not collide. Blue, cyan, navy, amber, red, lime, violet, magenta, neon green, tan-gold, orange, ember and glacier are all taken or forbidden.

**The open ground chosen is deep automotive lacquer green**, with the real protagonist being **platinum-white specular light**.

**Why green, and why this green:** dark green is a genuine automotive paint colour, which keeps the system honest; it goes almost black at depth, which a gloss system requires; it is cool enough to read as premium without being blue; and at this saturation it is unmistakably **not** the electric blue of bayside-mobile-detailing nor the amber-and-red of SD Prodigy. Critically, **the accent is a minority colour here** — roughly 5% of surface — whereas bayside uses blue at volume in gradients on bands, cards and CTAs. **Two sites can share a dark ground and still not be relatives if one is driven by hue and the other by light.** That is the separation, and it holds in a screenshot.

## Animations & gradients

The register is **wet, smooth and settled** — light moving across a polished surface, not a UI pulsing. Nothing bouncy, nothing neon, nothing that loops forever.

- **The specular rake — the signature motion.** On section reveal, a narrow high-contrast band of `--specular` sweeps **once** diagonally across the display type or the card row, 520ms, `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`, then stops and stays stopped. ⚠️ **Used at most four times on the homepage** — the GLAZE wordmark, the mobile-advantage band, the package cards, the radius map. ⛔ **It never loops.** A looping shine sweep is the single cheapest-looking effect available and it would wreck this.
- **Matte → gloss on the before/after slider.** As the handle moves, the revealed side gains saturation, contrast and the specular highlight; the hidden side loses them. Driven by the drag, not by a timer. **The one piece of real interactivity on the site, and it is also the brand.**
- **Reflection gradients** — beneath cards, the wordmark and key figures: a 40–60px mirrored fade at 12–18% opacity. **Static.** ⛔ They do not animate, shimmer or ripple.
- **Wet-edge section transitions** draw in left-to-right on reveal, 280ms — a 1px `--specular` line at 30% with a 4px bloom beneath it. ⛔ Not a glow, ⛔ not a neon rule, ⛔ no pulse.
- **Scroll reveals** — fade + 16px rise, 420ms, ease-out, 70ms stagger across a row. Calm.
- **Service cards** — image scale 1.03 max, 240ms, with the card's specular highlight shifting a few degrees. ⛔ No tilt, no 3D, no shadow bloom, no border glow.
- **Service-area chips** — `--jade-wash` fill on hover, 120ms. Twenty chips, so the motion has to be cheap and quiet.
- ⛔ **NO COUNT-UPS.** There is not one verified figure on this site to count up to — no years, no vehicles detailed, no square footage, no durability number. ⚠️ "20 areas" is the only countable fact and it renders as static text; animating it invites the next one to be invented.
- **The `[CONFIRM]` cells reveal with everything else** and are ⛔ **never** animated separately, highlighted, pulsed, shimmered or made to look pending. A gap sits there plainly; it does not perform.
- **Hover on fills** — `--jade` → `--jade-deep`, 120ms. 1px `--rule` → `--platinum` on card borders.
- **Always respect `prefers-reduced-motion`** — no specular rake, no wet-edge draw, no scroll reveals, no card scale, no chip transition. The before/after slider still drags (it is a control, not an animation) but loses its transition easing. Everything renders in final state, immediately.

**Gradients:** photo scrims only, two stops, one family — `--lacquer` → transparent at 180° over every dark photograph so type stays legible. One `--jade` → `--jade-deep` two-stop linear on the primary CTA, very shallow. The reflection gradients beneath elements. `--jade-wash` behind the MOST REQUESTED card and the active chip.

⛔ **No multi-hue gradients, no mesh, no rainbow, no glow, no bloom beyond the 4px wet edge, no chrome-gradient fills on type** (that is bayside's), ⛔ **no water droplets, no splashes, no spray arcs, no suds, no bubbles** (that is a car wash, and ref-01's water-splash hero is exactly what we are not doing), ⛔ no carbon fibre, no brushed-metal texture, no grunge, no distressing, no lens flare, no sparkle or star glints, no looping shine sweep.
